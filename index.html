Learn GitLab

GitLab taught as what it adds over git plus a host: planning, code review, CI/CD, a container registry and security scanning under one permission tree, and the trade-offs that come with one application. Penwood, a constructed five-person logistics-analytics team, moves its data pipelines onto GitLab.com's Free tier across every session, so everything in the build-alongs runs on a free account and every paid feature is shown with its tier. Six leader sessions take the platform decision from "one application, not six" to a 90-day migration plan. Eight builder sessions build Penwood's project, merge request, .gitlab-ci.yml, variables, rules and needs, environments and registry, and end at a bench that parses the real pipeline in your browser and prints its two clocks and its compute minutes.

Leader track 🧭 · six sessions, no code

For whoever chooses the platform, owns the group and pays for the minutes. Decide hosting and tier, set roles once, read a pipeline without running one, pick the controls that matter, and plan the move in three blocks.

One application, not six

What a git host leaves out and GitLab folds in, one tree of roles that inherit down, and the three costs of one application: one model of the work, one bill in compute minutes, one blast radius.

▶ Start here 🏗️ Leader session 2

SaaS or self-managed

GitLab.com, Self-Managed or Dedicated; hosted runners or your own; what the Free tier gives and withholds, with the dates on every number.

 🌳 Leader session 3

Groups, projects and permissions

Top-level group, subgroups, projects. Eight roles and the one rule about inheritance. Five seats on Free and the three ways out when the sixth person arrives.

 📈 Leader session 4

Reading a pipeline like a manager

Stages are a queue, needs are edges, rules decide what exists. Three clocks: wall, serial, compute minutes. Why a green pipeline with one job fewer is the one to ask about.

 🛡️ Leader session 5

Governance without friction

The protected branch and the merge request as the control point on Free; what Premium adds and when it is worth it; why masked is not secret.

 🗓️ Leader session 6

The 90-day migration plan

Three 30-day blocks: repo and board, the pipeline with the quota in view, registry and environments. The exit criteria and the day the tier decision is due.

Builder track 🔧 · eight sessions, a free account and the browser

Penwood's pipeline built one keyword at a time, in the order a team meets them, from the first project to a bench that measures the whole file.

🗂️ Builder session 1 · start here

Project, issues, boards

The group, subgroup and project whose URL is the tree; five people at the smallest role that works; labels, a board whose lists are labels, and what dragging a card really changes.

▶ Start here 🔀 Builder session 2

The merge request

Same idea as a pull request, different mechanics: approvals that are optional on Free, "pipelines must succeed", and a pipeline that belongs to the merge request itself.

 📄 Builder session 3

Your first .gitlab-ci.yml

Stages, jobs, script, image, default. The first green pipeline, 13 jobs in 30:08, and what each compute minute costs on Free.

 📦 Builder session 4

Variables, artifacts, cache

Where a variable comes from and which one wins; artifacts that move results forward and expire; a cache that is a hope, not a promise.

 🕸️ Builder session 5

Rules, needs and the DAG

First match wins and no match means not added; needs that skip the stage queue, 30:08 to 26:22; the job that is not there and the optional need that saves the pipeline.

 🚀 Builder session 6

Environments and deployments

An environment is a name on a job; stop it on purpose; the blocking manual gate on production, and the Premium version shown beside it.

 🧰 Builder session 7

Container registry and templates

The project's own registry at the project's own path, the job's own login, include then extend, and the templates GitLab ships.

 🎛️ Builder session 8

The pipeline DAG bench

Four rungs from 30:08 to 19:17, a stage-per-job layout that costs 479 minutes a month, a rules typo that drops the tests and stays green, and 1,803.8 compute minutes against 400.
