Learn GitLab · Builder session 1 of 8

Project, issues, boards

Before a single pipeline runs, a team needs a place for the code, a place for the work and a way to tell who may touch which. In GitLab those are one tree: a top-level group, a subgroup, a project, with roles granted once and inherited down. Penwood, a constructed five-person logistics-analytics team, builds that tree tonight on a GitLab.com Free account: the group penwood, the subgroup data, the project pipelines, five members with the smallest role that does their job, six labels, a board with three label lists, and the first six issues. Then you drag a card and watch what the board actually changes.

Part 1 · covers group, subgroup, project and the URL that mirrors them

The tree you create first

6 min live

The groups page says it plainly: "you use groups to manage one or more related projects at the same time". A top-level group is "created at the root"; subgroups nest below it, up to 20 levels with five or fewer recommended; projects hang off any of them. The URL is the tree: gitlab.com/penwood/data/pipelines. Visibility can only narrow as you go down, and on Free a new account gets three top-level groups.

Live Why one subgroup and not zero 3 min▶

Penwood could put pipelines straight under penwood. It adds the data subgroup for one reason the subgroups page gives: a subgroup inherits the parent's members and lets you grant a higher role below without touching the parent. Next year a finance subgroup can exist beside data with the same five people inheriting Reporter from the top and only two of them raised to Maintainer inside it. The page also says you "can't give a user a role in a subgroup that is lower than the roles the user has in parent groups", so the top of the tree is where the lowest roles belong. The recommendation to stay within five levels is about performance; Penwood uses two.

Self-study Visibility: private, internal, public 2 min▶

A group can be visible to anonymous users, to all signed-in users, or only to its members, and "Users cannot create a subgroup or project with a higher visibility level than that of the immediate parent group". Penwood's group is private, so everything under it is private or narrower. The free user limit page notes the trade: the five-user limit applies to private top-level groups, and public top-level groups are exempt. Making a data team's group public to escape the limit is a decision about data, not about GitLab; Leader session 3 takes it.

Real world

The container registry page says "The path of a container repository always matches the related project's repository path". Rename or move the project later and the image path moves with it. Penwood, constructed, picks penwood/data/pipelines once tonight knowing that Builder session 7 will push images to exactly that path. The tree is not a folder structure you tidy later; it is a set of addresses other systems will hold.

Part 2 · covers issues, labels and what a board does when you drag a card

Boards read labels

6 min live

A board in GitLab is not a separate store of state. Each list is a query over issues with a label, and the issue boards page says exactly what a drag does: "Moving an issue between lists removes the label from the list it came from and adds the label from the list it goes to". Understand that one sentence and you understand why a board can never disagree with the issue list.

Live Labels first, board second 3 min▶

Because a list is a label, the labels are the design. Penwood's constructed set is six: three for state (todo, doing, review) and three for area (pipeline, quality, docs). The state labels become the board's lists; the area labels filter it. The boards page says label lists are on every tier, that a project can have multiple boards on Free, and that a group gets one board on Free. Assignee lists and milestone lists are Premium and Ultimate; the Free way to see "what is Mei doing" is the issue list filtered by assignee.

Self-study Epics are Premium; the Free way is a label and a parent issue 2 min▶

The epics page carries a Premium and Ultimate badge and defines epics as a way to "coordinate and track large initiatives by organizing work items into a work hierarchy". On Free, Penwood groups its migration work with the area labels and one issue per block that links the others in its description. It is less elegant and entirely sufficient for five people and fourteen jobs. When the hierarchy itself becomes the problem, that is a signal for Leader session 5, not a reason to fake it with labels.

Real world

Constructed: Penwood's analyst edits issue #2 from the issue page and replaces todo with review by hand, skipping doing. On the board the card jumps two lists. Nobody dragged anything. That is the mechanism working, not a bug: there is one record, and the board is one of its views. Teams coming from tools where the board is the record expect the reverse and spend a week confused.

Part 3 · covers the names GitLab uses for things you already know

What GitLab calls it

5 min live

What a repository, an issue and a pull request are is taught in the GitHub course and GitHub for builders; nothing here reteaches them. What changes when you arrive at GitLab is mostly vocabulary, and one or two mechanisms behind the vocabulary.

Live The one rename that hides a mechanism 2 min▶

A merge request is the same idea as a pull request: a proposed change, a diff, a conversation, a pipeline result, a merge button. The merge requests page describes it as "a central location for your team to review code, have discussions, and track code changes". What differs is underneath: on Free, approvals exist but "are optional and don't prevent merging without approval", and a merge request can have its own pipeline type, which runs on the source branch and carries a merge request label. Both change how Penwood's pipeline is written. Session 2 takes them.

Real world

The protected branches page says "The default branch for your repository is protected by default", and that a new branch rule selects Maintainers for "Allowed to push and merge". Constructed: Penwood's Developer-role analyst clones the new project, commits straight to main and is refused. Nothing is misconfigured; the default is doing its job. The analyst opens a branch and a merge request instead, which is the loop every later session assumes.

Build-along

Penwood's tree, members, labels and board

★ 22 min · everyone builds

A free GitLab.com account is all you need. Every step below is a click path in the web UI as of 2026-10-29; menus move between releases, so if a name differs, search the settings page for the word. Penwood's values are constructed; use your own group name, since penwood is taken by whoever read this first.

Create the tree. New group, private visibility, name it (not penwood). Inside it, new subgroup data. Inside that, new project pipelines, blank, with a README so main exists. Check the URL in the address bar: it is the tree. Note that "Allowed to push and merge" on main already reads Maintainers; you did not set that.

★ Penwood's tree (constructed)group penwood private top level -> gitlab.com/penwood subgroup penwood/data private inherits -> gitlab.com/penwood/data project penwood/data/pipelines README on main -> gitlab.com/penwood/data/pipelines

Add the five people at the smallest role that does the job. Group members page: invite by username or email. Grant the low roles at the top and raise one person in the subgroup. Then open the group's usage or billing page and read the seat count.

★ Penwood's members (constructed) · roles as the permissions page defines themwhere who role why this role and not a higher one group penwood data lead Owner someone must be able to delete and change visibility group penwood analytics eng (Mei) Developer pushes to branches, opens merge requests, runs pipelines group penwood analyst Developer same loop; cannot change CI/CD settings group penwood finance partner Reporter reads code and reports, files issues; cannot push subgroup data analytics eng (Raj) Maintainer manages branch rules and CI/CD settings for data only seat count: 5 unique users of 5 (private top-level group, Free, as of 2026-10-29)

Create six labels. Project labels page. Three for state, three for area. Keep the names lower case and short; they will appear on every card and in every filter.

★ Penwood's labels (constructed)state: todo doing review area: pipeline quality docs

Make the board. Project boards page: create a board, add three lists, one per state label, in order. The open and closed lists come for free. Create the six issues below with their labels, and watch them land in the right list without being placed.

★ Penwood's first six issues (constructed)#1 lint rules for SQL models review pipeline #2 dedupe the orders feed todo pipeline #3 quality thresholds for the daily totals todo quality #4 first .gitlab-ci.yml with lint and build doing pipeline #5 nightly backfill job on a schedule todo pipeline #6 runbook for the staging deploy todo docs

Drag #2 from todo to doing, then open the issue. Its labels now read doing and pipeline; todo is gone. Now edit #3 on its issue page and swap todo for review, then look at the board. The card moved without a drag. Say out loud what the board stores: nothing.

★ What you should see, roughlyboard after the drag: todo 2 (#3 #5 #6 -> 3) doing 2 (#2 #4) review 1 (#1) issue #2 labels: doing, pipeline after editing #3: todo 2 (#5 #6) doing 2 review 2 (#1 #3) your numbers will differ if you created different issues
✗ The step to skipInviting a sixth person "just to look". The free user limit page: "Top-level private namespaces with more than five users are placed in a read-only state." Every surface under the group stops accepting writes, and the limit cannot be raised on Free. Give them a link to a public project, or decide the tier first.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Builder session 1 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · An issue carries todo and pipeline. You drag its card from the todo list to the doing list. What is stored afterwards?

"Moving an issue between lists removes the label from the list it came from and adds the label from the list it goes to." The board is a view over labels; the area label is untouched because no list is defined by it.

2 · Penwood's finance partner needs to read the quality report and open issues when a number looks wrong. Which role, granted where?

Guest cannot access the repository, so no report. Developer and Maintainer can push, which the partner does not need and the tree would inherit downward. Reporter is the smallest role that does the job, granted once at the top.

3 · The same contractor is a member of three projects under Penwood's private top-level group and of nothing else. How many of the five Free seats do they use, and what happens at six unique users?

The limit counts unique users across the group, its subgroups and its projects. Over five, the docs say the namespace is "placed in a read-only state", and the limit cannot be raised on Free. Re-verify the page before relying on it.
